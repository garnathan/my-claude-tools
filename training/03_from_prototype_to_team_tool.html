From the living room to the dog park.

You wouldn't take a pup to the park without a leash. Don't share your app without these.

01 / 9

Security & data

Has the pup had its shots?

Who can use it, what information it touches, and where that goes. Internal tools can still hurt the company.

  • Who can log in — and would you spot it if the wrong person did?
  • Could you delete a user's data if they asked?
Try this prompt →

List every way someone can reach my app, and which require sign-in. Trace what user info we save, and flag anywhere we keep passwords, keys, or personal info. Plan a sign-in lockdown and a "delete my data" button.

02 / 9

Cost

Who's paying for the kibble?

Mostly AI usage, plus servers and storage. "Cheap when it's just me" is how surprise bills happen.

  • What does one person cost per day — times 100?
  • Will an alarm warn you before the bill gets scary?
Try this prompt →

Estimate per-use cost. Project the monthly bill at 100 people × 20 uses a day. Name the 3 biggest spenders. Plan a daily limit per person, a total spend alarm, and a safety switch for runaway usage.

03 / 9

Compliance

Is the pup licensed for the park?

The boring rules: who signs off, what software you can use, where data can live. Boring until it isn't.

  • Does this need a security sign-off before more people use it?
  • Are there countries or kinds of data that change the rules?
Try this prompt →

List every outside library my app uses. Flag any with licenses Oracle doesn't allow. Identify what kinds of data it handles, then draft the security-review questions I should expect — with my best-guess answers.

04 / 9

Critical dependencies

Does the pup need its blanket?

What does your app need to do its job? If it depends on what it's meant to help — you've got a chicken-and-egg problem.

  • If something your app relies on goes down, does the app still open?
  • Worst case — blanket and pup missing — what breaks?
Try this prompt →

List every outside service my app uses. For each, tell me what happens if it disappears, whether there's a backup, and any chicken-and-egg risks. Plan how to keep the app useful when they break.

06 / 9

Monitoring

Can you tell when the pup's sick?

The difference between "broken for an hour" and "broken for a week" — until a user complains.

  • Would you know within minutes, or only when a user complains?
  • Does anyone actually get notified — or does it just sit there?
Try this prompt →

Tell me how I'd find out my app broke today. List what it writes down, measures, and alerts on. Plan structured logs, an "is it alive?" check, a 3-signal dashboard, and an alert that pings someone's phone.

07 / 9

Scalability

What happens at the dog park?

Behaving with one walker is easy. Behaving with fifty other dogs around is the actual test.

  • Have you tested it with more than one person at the same time?
  • Will it feel fast with 10× the people? 100×?
Try this prompt →

Walk through what happens at 1, 10, and 100 people using my app at once. Find the slowest part — database, memory, anything waiting in line — and plan the fix, plus the smallest changes that unlock 10× more people.

08 / 9

Hosting

One pup, or the whole kennel?

Does each person run their own copy, or do they share one running somewhere central?

  • If shared, who pushes updates — and how often?
  • Which setup actually fits what this tool is for?
Try this prompt →

Tell me if this is better as a private copy per person, one shared version everyone visits, or a mix — and why. Plan how updates reach people, how I rotate passwords and keys safely, and how I undo a bad release.

09 / 9

Maintenance

Who walks the pup on holiday?

When you're on PTO, who keeps it alive? When you leave the team, who inherits it?

  • Are there written instructions to restart, fix, or change passwords?
  • If you got hit by a bus tomorrow, would this die with you?
Try this prompt →

Pretend I'm leaving Oracle next month. Write the instructions my replacement needs: how to restart, release a fix, change passwords and keys, find on-call, and access every part of the system. Flag anything only in my head.
